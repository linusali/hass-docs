== What is TIS Control, and what did we replace?

Several villas in our community, including yours, were built with a proprietary
control panel system called *TIS Control*. It's a closed system from one
manufacturer - it works, but you can't see how it works, can't easily add cheaper
devices to it, and depend entirely on that one company for support and spare parts.

In one villa, we replaced the "brain" of that system with *Home Assistant* - a free,
open-source home automation platform used by millions of people worldwide. The
wiring behind your walls, your light switches, and your air-conditioning units don't
need to change. What changes is *what is in charge* of them.

== What stays the same, what changes

* Your light switches on the wall still work as switches.
* Your air-conditioning still works from its usual controls.
* You gain the ability to also control things from a phone app, automate them
  (e.g. "turn off all lights at midnight"), and combine devices in ways the closed
  TIS system didn't allow.
* You are no longer dependent on one manufacturer for support, updates, or spare
  parts - the software is free, open-source, and maintained by a large community.
* Nothing about your legal ownership, warranty, or community agreements changes
  automatically - check with community management/HOA before making changes to
  shared systems.

== The big picture

image::architecture-simple.svg[How the pieces fit together,700,,align="center"]

In plain words:

* A small computer (about the size of a deck of cards, called a *Raspberry Pi*)
  becomes the new "brain" of your villa, replacing the old TIS Control panel.
* That brain talks wirelessly, using a technology called *Zigbee*, to your lights,
  switches, and similar devices.
* It also talks over your home network to your villa's own air-conditioning
  system (each villa has its own dedicated AC controller and unit - it isn't
  shared with neighbours), so you keep controlling the AC the same way you always
  did (just from the new system instead of the old one).
* None of this needs the internet to work day-to-day inside your home. The internet
  is only involved if you *choose* to turn on the optional ability to control your
  home from your phone while you're away - and that step is optional, has some
  security trade-offs, and is explained in the technical guide.

== What you would need (shopping list, in plain terms)

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Item | What it's for

| A small computer ("Raspberry Pi 5")
| Runs the Home Assistant software - this becomes the new "brain".

| A memory card or small SSD, and a power supply
| Storage and power for the small computer.

| A Zigbee "coordinator" (a small network gateway device)
| Lets the brain talk wirelessly to your lights, switches, and sensors.

| Zigbee-compatible switches/relays/sensors for each room
| The actual devices being controlled - these may replace or sit behind your
  existing wall switches (electrician work).

| A network cable or reliable Wi-Fi
| Connects the small computer to your home network/router.
|===

Exact models and where to buy them are listed in the
link:guide-technical.adoc[Technical Guide].

== How to get started, at a high level

. Read this guide fully, and skim the link:guide-technical.adoc[Technical Guide] so
  you know roughly what's involved.
. Decide, with community management/HOA if relevant, whether you're doing this yourself,
  with a technical neighbour's help, or hiring someone.
. Get the hardware from the shopping list above.
. Have a qualified electrician handle anything involving wiring, wall boxes, or the
  distribution board.
. Follow the link:guide-technical.adoc[Technical Guide] to install and configure the
  software, or have your technical neighbour do it with you watching/learning.
. Add your devices one room at a time - there's no need to convert the whole
  villa in one day.
. Optionally, once everything works locally, consider whether you want remote
  access from your phone - this is covered, with its risks, in the technical guide.

== Getting help

This is neighbour-to-neighbour support, not a paid service.

* Ask the neighbour(s) who've already done this before experimenting on your own,
  especially anything touching systems shared with the wider community (e.g.
  common-area wiring). Your villa's own AC and Zigbee devices are private to you,
  but check first if you're ever unsure whether something is exclusively yours.
* Take it step by step - you don't have to convert everything at once.
* If something feels unsafe or you're unsure, stop and ask - either a neighbour or a
  qualified professional (electrician for wiring, community management/HOA for
  shared systems).
