[WARNING]
.⚠️ WARNING: Electrical safety - please read first
====
Parts of this project involve wiring connected to *mains / line voltage electricity*.

* Working with live electrical wiring can cause **serious injury, fire, or death**.
* Any work inside a wall box, distribution board, relay panel, or anything touching
  mains wiring *must* be performed, or at minimum inspected and signed off, by a
  qualified electrician familiar with your local electrical code.
* The standard conversion described here needs **no electrical work at all** -
  the existing switches and sensors stay untouched in their wall boxes and are
  re-paired over the air. If you ever find yourself about to open a wall box,
  stop and call an electrician.

[#_architecture]
== Architecture

image::architecture-technical.svg[Technical architecture diagram,900,,align="center"]

Components:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Component | Role

| Raspberry Pi 5, running Home Assistant OS (HAOS)
| Host for everything below. Runs Home Assistant Core plus add-ons in isolated
  containers.

| Home Assistant Core
| Automations, dashboards, users/auth, and remote-access endpoint.

| Mosquitto add-on
| Local MQTT broker. Zigbee2MQTT publishes device state here; Home Assistant
  subscribes to it via the official MQTT integration.

| Zigbee2MQTT add-on
| Talks to the Zigbee coordinator, exposes every paired Zigbee device over MQTT.

| SMLIGHT SLZB-06M
| Zigbee 3.0 coordinator. Unlike a plain USB dongle, it's network-attached
  (Ethernet or Wi-Fi, with PoE support), so Zigbee2MQTT connects to it over TCP
  instead of needing USB passthrough into the HAOS VM - simpler and more reliable
  on a Raspberry Pi host.

| Existing TIS Zigbee devices
| The light switches and motion sensors TIS installed are native Zigbee devices.
  They are re-paired from the TIS network to your own coordinator - no new
  hardware, no rewiring, no cost.

| `hass-tis-vrf-ac` custom integration
| Community-written Home Assistant `climate` platform
  (https://github.com/linusali/hass-tis-vrf-ac[source]) that talks HTTP directly to
  the villa's own TIS VRF air-conditioning controller, so the original AC hardware
  keeps working under Home Assistant instead of the TIS Control panel.

| TIS VRF AC controller
| Existing proprietary hardware, kept in place. Dedicated to this villa only - each
  villa has its own controller and AC unit, not a system shared across the
  community. Reachable on the villa's LAN over HTTP, optionally with HTTP Basic
  Auth.

| Remote access (Tailscale, or alternatives)
| Optional secure path to reach Home Assistant from outside the villa - see
  <<_remote_access>>.
|===

Everything except the optional remote-access path works with **no internet
connection**.

== Bill of materials

The switches, motion sensors, and wiring that TIS Control used to drive are not
on this list - they are already in your villa, they are already Zigbee, and they
get re-paired rather than replaced. **The re-use is free.** What you buy is only
the new brain and its radio:

[cols="2,3,1", options="header"]
|===
| Item | Notes | Approx. role

| Raspberry Pi 5, **8 GB RAM** (64-bit)
| The HAOS host. The 8 GB model gives comfortable headroom for add-ons; use the
  official 27 W USB-C power supply.
| The brain

| microSD card, **64 GB or larger**
| A2-rated card from a reputable brand. (An SSD via USB is a worthwhile future
  upgrade for longevity, but a good SD card is fine to start.)
| Storage

| SMLIGHT **SLZB-06M**
| Zigbee 3.0 coordinator with Ethernet/Wi-Fi/USB and PoE options. Connect it to
  your LAN via Ethernet rather than USB - it runs independently of the Pi.
| Zigbee radio

| Ethernet cable(s)
| For the Pi and (recommended) the SLZB-06M. Wired beats Wi-Fi for reliability.
| Network
|===

That's the whole list - roughly the cost of a mid-range phone accessory haul,
with no recurring fees.

== Installing Home Assistant OS on the Raspberry Pi 5

. Download the official Raspberry Pi 5 HAOS image from the
  https://www.home-assistant.io/installation/raspberrypi[Home Assistant installation
  page].
. Flash it to your SD card using
  https://www.home-assistant.io/common-tasks/os/#installing-home-assistant-operating-system[Raspberry
  Pi Imager] or `balenaEtcher`.
. Boot the Pi with it connected to your router via Ethernet.
. Browse to `\http://homeassistant.local:8123` from a device on the same network and
  complete the onboarding wizard (create your admin account - use a strong,
  unique password).
. Apply any pending HAOS/Core updates from *Settings -> System -> Updates* before
  continuing.

== Setting up Zigbee2MQTT with the SLZB-06M

. Connect the SLZB-06M to your network (Ethernet recommended) and give it a fixed
  IP, either via a DHCP reservation on your router or a static IP on the device
  itself. This guide uses `192.0.2.50` as a placeholder - use your own address.
. Install the *Mosquitto broker* add-on from *Settings -> Add-ons -> Add-on Store*,
  start it, and create a dedicated MQTT user for Zigbee2MQTT (do not reuse your HA
  admin account).
. Install the *Zigbee2MQTT* add-on from the add-on store.
. Configure the add-on (via its *Configuration* tab, or `configuration.yaml` inside
  the add-on) to point at the coordinator over the network rather than USB:
+
[source,yaml]
----
serial:
  port: tcp://192.0.2.50:6638   # <-- replace with your SLZB-06M's own IP
  adapter: ezsp

mqtt:
  base_topic: zigbee2mqtt
  server: mqtt://core-mosquitto:1883
  user: <MQTT_USERNAME>
  password: <MQTT_PASSWORD_REDACTED>
----
. Start the add-on and open its web UI (via the *Zigbee2MQTT* panel in the HA
  sidebar) to confirm the coordinator connects.
. In Home Assistant, add the *MQTT* integration (*Settings -> Devices & Services ->
  Add Integration -> MQTT*) pointing at the same broker - Zigbee2MQTT devices will
  then appear automatically via MQTT discovery.

== Re-pairing the existing TIS switches and motion sensors

This is the heart of the zero-cost story: the TIS wall switches and motion
sensors are standard Zigbee devices currently paired to the TIS network. Moving
them to yours is a software operation done from the room, not the wall box.

. In the Zigbee2MQTT web UI, enable *Permit join*.
. Put the device into pairing mode. The exact button-press or power-cycle
  sequence varies by device model - ask in the WhatsApp group; the neighbours
  who've converted already know the sequence for each TIS device type in our
  villas, and this section will grow as each model is documented.
. Watch the Zigbee2MQTT log: the device joins, gets interviewed, and appears
  with its capabilities.
. Rename it immediately with a clear, consistent scheme (e.g.
  `living_room_ceiling_light`, `hallway_motion`) - this pays off enormously when
  writing automations later.
. Repeat, one room at a time. There's no rush, and devices still paired to TIS
  keep working under TIS until you move them.

TIP: Keep the coordinator away from Wi-Fi routers/USB 3.0 devices where possible;
2.4 GHz interference is the most common cause of a flaky Zigbee network.

== Controlling the villa's VRF air-conditioning

The https://github.com/linusali/hass-tis-vrf-ac[`hass-tis-vrf-ac`] integration is a
custom `climate` platform that talks HTTP directly to the TIS VRF AC controller that
was already on your network, so the original AC hardware keeps working under Home
Assistant. Each villa has its own controller and AC unit - this touches nothing
outside your own home.

. Confirm the TIS VRF AC controller's IP address on your LAN (check your router's
  client list, or the original TIS Control installation documentation).
. Install the integration, following the
  https://github.com/linusali/hass-tis-vrf-ac[repository's own `INSTALLATION.md`] -
  either via HACS (custom repository) or by copying `custom_components/ac_vrf` into
  your HAOS `config/custom_components` folder (accessible via the *Samba* or
  *File editor* add-on, or SSH).
. Restart Home Assistant, then add the platform to your configuration. For example,
  in `configuration.yaml`:
+
[source,yaml]
----
climate:
  - platform: ac_vrf
    host: 192.0.2.251                 # <-- replace with your AC controller's IP
    username: <AC_CONTROLLER_USERNAME>  # optional, only if your controller requires auth
    password: <AC_CONTROLLER_PASSWORD_REDACTED>  # optional, can be blank if unset

logger:
  default: info
  logs:
    custom_components.ac_vrf: debug     # helpful while getting this working, remove after
----
. Restart Home Assistant again and confirm a new climate entity appears for each AC
  zone/unit. Test basic on/off and temperature-setpoint changes before wiring it
  into any automations.
. Check the repository's own
  https://github.com/linusali/hass-tis-vrf-ac/blob/main/TROUBLESHOOTING.md[`TROUBLESHOOTING.md`]
  and the included connection-test script if the controller doesn't respond -
  it's usually an IP, firewall, or auth mismatch rather than a Home Assistant
  problem.

[#_remote_access]
== Remote access

Controlling your villa from outside your home Wi-Fi is genuinely useful (AC on
before you arrive, checking things while travelling), so this section is part of
the standard build rather than an afterthought. There are two good free
approaches; **the VPN approach is recommended** because it exposes nothing to the
internet.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| | VPN mesh (recommended) | DuckDNS + Let's Encrypt

| How it works
| Your phone and the Pi join a private encrypted network (WireGuard-based);
  you reach HA as if you were at home.
| Your router forwards port 443 to HA, protected by TLS and your HA login.

| Exposure to internet
| **None** - no open ports.
| One HTTPS port open to the whole internet.

| Setup difficulty
| Easy - install two apps, log in.
| Moderate - DNS, certificates, router port-forwarding.

| Works behind CG-NAT
| Yes.
| No (needs a real public IP).

| Cost
| Free tier is ample for a household.
| Free.
|===

=== Option A (recommended): Tailscale

https://tailscale.com[Tailscale] is a zero-configuration WireGuard mesh - the
easiest route by far.

. Install the *Tailscale* add-on from the Home Assistant add-on store and start
  it.
. Open the add-on's Web UI and log in with a free Tailscale account (Google/
  Microsoft/GitHub sign-in supported).
. Install the Tailscale app on your phone and log in to the same account.
. That's it - from anywhere, open `\http://<the-pi's-tailscale-name>:8123` (or its
  `100.x.y.z` Tailscale IP) in the HA companion app.

NOTE: https://netbird.io[NetBird] is an equally good open-source alternative with
a similar flow (add-on/agent on the Pi, app on the phone, same account on both).
If you prefer fully self-hosted infrastructure or hit a Tailscale limitation, use
it the same way.

=== Option B: DuckDNS + Let's Encrypt (direct exposure)

The classic free path documented by Home Assistant, for those who want a plain
`https://` URL that works from any browser without a VPN app.

[CAUTION]
====
This forwards a port from the internet to your Pi. Only choose it if you
understand the trade-off, and follow the hardening steps below.
====

. Register a free hostname at https://www.duckdns.org[duckdns.org], e.g.
  `<your-subdomain>.duckdns.org`, and note the DuckDNS token (treat it like a
  password).
. Install the official *DuckDNS* add-on and configure it:
+
[source,yaml]
----
lets_encrypt:
  accept_terms: true
  certfile: fullchain.pem
  keyfile: privkey.pem
token: <DUCKDNS_TOKEN_REDACTED>
domains:
  - <your-subdomain>.duckdns.org
aliases: []
----
. Point Home Assistant at the certificate in `configuration.yaml`:
+
[source,yaml]
----
http:
  ssl_certificate: /ssl/fullchain.pem
  ssl_key: /ssl/privkey.pem
----
. Forward external port `443` to the Pi's port `8123` on your router.
. Set *Settings -> System -> Network -> Home Assistant URL* to
  `\https://<your-subdomain>.duckdns.org`, restart, and test from mobile data.

Hardening (mandatory if you choose Option B):

* Strong, unique admin password and
  https://www.home-assistant.io/docs/authentication/two-factor-auth/[two-factor
  authentication] on every account.
* A separate non-admin account for day-to-day household use.
* Enable the failed-login IP ban threshold in the `http:` configuration.
* Keep HAOS, Core, and add-ons updated.
* If you later switch to Tailscale, remove the port forward and disable the
  DuckDNS add-on.

== Backups and updates

* Enable Home Assistant's built-in
  https://www.home-assistant.io/common-tasks/general/#back-up-and-restore-your-configuration[automatic
  backups] (*Settings -> System -> Backups*) before making significant changes, and
  before every major HAOS/Core update.
* Store at least one backup copy off the Pi itself (network share, cloud storage,
  USB drive) so an SD-card failure doesn't take your only backup with it.
* Before re-pairing devices in bulk, take a fresh backup - the Zigbee network
  state lives in Zigbee2MQTT's data and is included in a full HA backup.

== Troubleshooting

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
| Symptom | Likely cause / where to look

| Zigbee2MQTT add-on won't connect to the coordinator
| Wrong IP/port for the SLZB-06M, or it's not reachable on the network. Check its
  own web UI (reachable directly by browsing to its IP) and the add-on logs.

| A TIS device won't pair
| It may still be joined to the TIS network - it must be removed/reset first
  (pairing-mode sequence varies by model; ask in the WhatsApp group). Also check
  *Permit join* is actually enabled and the device is within range.

| Zigbee devices randomly drop offline
| Usually 2.4 GHz interference (Wi-Fi routers, USB 3.0 cables/hubs) or the
  coordinator being too far from devices. Try relocating the coordinator.

| `ac_vrf` climate entity doesn't appear
| Check `Settings -> System -> Logs` for `custom_components.ac_vrf` errors, verify
  the controller's IP is correct and reachable (`ping`), and see the integration's
  own
  https://github.com/linusali/hass-tis-vrf-ac/blob/main/TROUBLESHOOTING.md[TROUBLESHOOTING.md].

| Can't reach HA remotely via Tailscale
| Confirm both the add-on and the phone app are logged in to the *same* Tailscale
  account, and the add-on shows a `100.x.y.z` address in its log.

| Can't reach HA remotely via DuckDNS
| Confirm the port forward is active (online port-checker from outside), the
  DuckDNS add-on log shows successful cert issuance, and your ISP isn't using
  carrier-grade NAT (which blocks inbound port forwarding - use Tailscale
  instead in that case).
|===

[#_roadmap]
== Roadmap: RS485 sensor bus integration

The villas carry an *RS485 bus* connecting additional built-in hardware that the
TIS panel used but the current conversion doesn't reach yet:

* additional **motion sensors** beyond the Zigbee ones
* **water-usage** metering
* **power-usage** metering

The plan is to bridge this bus into Home Assistant (typically via an RS485-to-
Ethernet/USB gateway plus a Modbus or custom integration, depending on what
protocol analysis reveals). This is under investigation in the villa that did the
first conversion - findings, wiring notes, and configuration will be added to
this guide and the automation library as they solidify. If you have RS485 or
protocol-reversing experience, this is the most valuable place to contribute.
